01 — About

Interfaces for the warehouse floor, the delivery route, and the station manager’s phone.

I’m a mobile engineering lead with 11 years across Flutter, native Android, and React Native, including more than five years shipping Flutter. The job is the screen, the architecture under it, and the release that gets both into people’s hands.

At ElasticRun I led an eight-person team across warehouse execution, last-mile delivery, partner operations, and vendor management. Those products support 5,000+ delivery associates and 1,000+ warehouse employees, on a network of about 100,000 deliveries a day.

I still write the UI. Planning, reviews, performance, and production ownership stay attached to the work — from the first layout to the build that ships. AI-assisted workflows, including Gemini, speed the modernization. The eye that signs off is still mine.

I’m interested in staff and principal mobile roles, mobile architecture, and platform engineering with product companies and global teams. Open to relocation.

How I treat the UI

The interface is the product people actually touch.

01

The next action is obvious

Putaway, pick lists, and delivery runs are used on the move. Hierarchy, touch targets, and status have to read in a glance.

02

It survives the network

Ordering, tasking, and field work stay usable offline. Local storage and sync are part of the interface, not a backend detail.

03

Polish ships with the release

Performance, stability, and the visual layer go out together. A smoother screen that misses the train is unfinished.

04

Scale shows up in the details

Forms, empty states, and error copy used by thousands of people a day get the same care as the hero flow.
